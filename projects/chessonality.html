PROJECT NOTES / ACTIVE EXPERIMENT

Chessonality.

Trying to describe how someone actually plays chess.

Most chess analysis tools tell you whether a move was good or bad. I wanted to investigate a different question: what patterns keep showing up in a particular player's decisions?

What I'm analyzing

The project examines chess games with engine-assisted move evaluation and aggregates features across a player's games: opening repertoire, opening/middlegame/endgame performance, clock usage when available, and recurring types of mistakes.

I'm also experimenting with interpretable player archetypes. They can make the results more approachable, but a catchy label should never be confused with a validated psychological description.

The difficult part

Small samples are noisy. Playing strength and time control change the picture. A player might look "aggressive" because they happen to enter sharp openings, not because they're uniquely risk-taking. I'm working on larger samples and peer-group calibration so the comparisons are more meaningful.

Current state

This is a work in progress. The data pipeline and calibration are evolving, and I treat the output as an analysis aid rather than a final judgment of someone's playing style.
